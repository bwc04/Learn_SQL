Course step #3: Group By and having

The GROUP BY clause and the HAVING clause are typically used in combination.
The purpose of GROUP BY and HAVING is to ignore groups that don't meet a certain condition
Take this query for example:

    SELECT 'Ads Revenue _Mn_', COUNT('Date')
    FROM youtube.youtube_ads_revenue
    GROUP BY 'Ads Revenue _Mn_'
    HAVING COUNT('Date') > 1;

This query uses GROUP BY to create a group based of the ads revenue. COUNT counts
the number of days in date with the same ads revenue. HAVING allows us to filter
out any ad revenue counts that are distinct. Now we only have a group of ad revenues
that are seen across multiple days.

* Since GROUP BY is typically used with a aggregate function, GROUP BY has to include all 
columns that were not passed to a aggregate function. 

    SELECT author, series, COUNT(id)
    FROM writtenWork.books
    GROUP BY author
    HAVING COUNT(id) > 3;

* This query finds the group of authors and book series that they have written where the series had 
more then 3 sequels. This query will unfortunately not work because series is not included in the 
GROUP BY.
